# Intake interview

Ask in small batches (3-4 questions at a time, with options where you can guess the answer). Write the answers into the spec as you go. Do not start code before the dates, players and production are clear.

## 1. The occasion
- Who plays? A friend group, a club, a youth movement, a family? How well do they know board games?
- When and where? The event dates. A table in a holiday house, a café, a tent? How wide is the table (the board must fit and be readable from every seat)?
- How long should a game take? A fixed number of rounds or an agreed end time ("we stop at midnight, the round that is running is the last")?
- Is there something the game must feel like (a running gag, a theme, their region, an old app they want on paper)?

## 2. Players
- How many exactly, and what if one is absent or one extra comes? Teams or individuals? Which team setups (3-3-2, 3-2-2, 3-3-3)?
- Does the designer play along? (Then there is no game master and the rules must be self-service.)
- Anyone colour blind? (Then team colours get a colour-blindness test and a shape per team.)

## 3. Real-world input
- Is there an input from outside the game: drinks, steps, quiz answers, minigames? How is it counted on paper (empties, tally marks)?
- Normal pace and heavy pace per person per hour? It must help, but the heaviest drinker must not win automatically.
- Drinking rules or sips inside the game: yes or no? (Often no: the game rewards, it does not force.)

## 4. Production
- Print shop: which one, what do they accept (PDF, CMYK, bleed), card size, can they cut cards, deadline and lead time? Ask the designer to ask them early.
- Home printer: A4 only? Heavy paper? Sticker paper?
- 3D printer (type, bed size, filament colours)? Spray paint colours actually available?
- Budget, and what they already have (dice, poker chips, sleeves).

## 5. Language and house style
- Language and tone of everything players see (e.g. Dutch, Flemish tone). The words for key things ("flesje", never "pint").
- House style: punctuation they dislike, phrases they always or never use, how they write numbers and first place.
- Names of friends on cards, inside jokes, avatars or photos? (Keep them out of anything shared publicly.)

## 6. What must not be in it
- No app during play? No game master? No hand cards, no titles, no player elimination? Things from earlier versions that were cut?
- Lettering or logos to avoid.

## 7. Working agreement
- How do they want to see progress (the task list, a shared project, messages)?
- Who pushes to GitHub, who prints, who talks to the print shop?
- Which choices do they want to make themselves (usually: every rule, every text, colours) and which may you decide (code structure, sim details)?
